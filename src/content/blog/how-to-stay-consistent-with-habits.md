Motivation is high on day one and unreliable by day twenty. Consistency comes from designing the habit so that it does not rely on motivation.

## 1. Shrink it until it is easy

If a habit feels heavy, make it smaller. "Run 5 km" becomes "put on running shoes and step outside". "Meditate 20 minutes" becomes "three slow breaths". You can always do more. The goal of the small version is to protect the chain on hard days.

## 2. Use a clear cue

A habit needs a trigger. Anchor it to something you already do: *after I pour my coffee, I write three lines.* If-then plans like this help: a 2006 meta-analysis of 94 studies found that people who made specific plans did better at following through than people who only set goals.

## 3. Never miss twice

Missing one day barely changes anything. Missing two starts a new pattern. Make this your rule: if you miss a day, the next day is non-negotiable, even in its smallest form.

## 4. Plan for obstacles

List the two things most likely to derail you, then write the if-then answer:

- *If I am travelling, then I will do the two-minute version in the hotel.*
- *If I am too tired at night, then I will do it before dinner instead.*

## 5. Measure completion, not perfection

A strict streak punishes you for one slip. Completion rate does not. Thirty days with 26 done is a strong 87%. Use the [consistency calculator](/habit-consistency-calculator/) and the [streak calculator](/habit-streak-calculator/) to see both numbers.

## 6. Make it visible

Put a printed tracker where you will see it, or keep one habit on your phone's first screen. What you see, you remember. Try a [printable weekly tracker](/printable-habit-tracker/).

## 7. Limit your list

Consistency drops as the list grows. Three habits you do nearly every day beat ten you do occasionally.

## 8. Expect plateaus

Research on habit formation has found a very wide range in how long a new behavior takes to feel automatic. A well-known University College London study of 96 people found it ranged from 18 to 254 days, with a median of 66. Do not judge yourself on a 21-day myth.

## 9. Review weekly

Every Sunday, check what got in the way and make one small adjustment. See [how to track habits effectively](/blog/how-to-track-habits-effectively/).

## When you fall off

1. Do the smallest version today.
2. Ask why, without judging.
3. Change one thing about the plan.
4. Keep tracking, because the record helps you restart.
