A printed habit tracker works because it sits where you can see it. The trick is choosing a layout that fits the habits you have, then keeping it simple enough to fill in daily.

## The four templates most people need

### Weekly tracker

Seven columns, a handful of rows and room for notes. Best for **two to six habits** and for beginners, because a week is long enough to see a pattern and short enough to fix one. It also builds in a weekly review, which is where most improvement happens.

### Monthly tracker

One row per habit and one column per day. Best for **spotting long-run patterns** and keeping a visible chain. It packs more on a page, so boxes are smaller. Print it in landscape.

### 30-day challenge sheet

Thirty numbered circles for a single habit. Best for **a focused sprint**, such as drinking more water or stretching daily. It has a clear finish line, which is motivating.

### Daily routine schedule

Morning, afternoon and evening blocks with times and tick boxes. Best for **structuring a day** rather than tracking individual habits.

## Layout styles

- **Grid:** ruled cells, easiest to read.
- **Minimal:** light lines, good for a journal or planner.
- **Bold header:** a colored band and tinted rows, good for a wall or fridge.
- **Dots:** a softer look for filling in with a pen.

## Printing tips

1. Choose **100% or "Actual size"** so boxes stay square.
2. Print **monthly sheets in landscape**.
3. Use US Letter in the United States and Canada, A4 elsewhere.
4. Slip the sheet into a clear sleeve and use a dry-erase marker to reuse it.

## Make it stick

- Put it where you will see it daily: the fridge, your desk, next to the coffee maker.
- Keep a pen on a string or taped to the page.
- Mark a missed day with a dash, not a scribble. It is data, not a failure.
- Do a five-minute review every Sunday. See [how to track habits effectively](/blog/how-to-track-habits-effectively/).

## When paper is not enough

Paper cannot remind you and does not calculate streaks. If you want that, try the [free online tracker](/online-habit-tracker/) or [an iPhone app](/habit-tracker-for-iphone/). Many people use both: paper for visibility, an app for reminders.
