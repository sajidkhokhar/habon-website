A morning routine is a short, repeatable sequence that you do after waking up. The best ones are boring, small and forgiving. They are not a 5 a.m. performance.

## Step 1: Decide what the routine is for

Pick one main purpose:

- **Energy:** water, light, movement.
- **Focus:** plan the day before opening email.
- **Calm:** a few minutes of stillness before the rush.
- **Getting out the door on time:** fewer decisions, less scrambling.

A routine with one purpose is easier to design and easier to keep.

## Step 2: Start with three steps

Choose three actions, no more. For example:

1. Drink a glass of water.
2. Stretch for five minutes.
3. Write your top three tasks.

Run that for two weeks before adding anything. Adding is easy. Removing steps after a routine collapses is harder.

## Step 3: Anchor it to something fixed

Link the first step to something that already happens: your alarm, getting out of bed, starting the coffee. The if-then format works well: *"When I put my feet on the floor, I drink a glass of water."* A 2006 meta-analysis of 94 studies on these if-then "implementation intentions" found they helped people follow through on goals compared with having the goal alone.

## Step 4: Prepare the night before

The best morning routines start the evening before. Lay out clothes, fill your water bottle, put your keys in the same place and write tomorrow's top tasks. See [how to build an evening routine](/blog/how-to-build-an-evening-routine/).

## Step 5: Set times, not vague windows

"7:15 stretch" is easier to act on than "sometime in the morning". Build a rough schedule from your wake time, leaving buffer. The [morning routine generator](/morning-routine-generator/) builds one from your wake time, available minutes and focus, and you can refine it in the [daily routine planner](/daily-routine-planner/).

## Step 6: Keep a two-minute fallback

On a rough morning, do the minimum: water and one stretch. Keeping the chain alive on hard days is what makes the routine automatic.

## Step 7: Keep screens for later

Many people find that opening email or social media first makes the morning reactive. Try 30 minutes of delay. Treat it as an experiment: if it helps, keep it.

## A sample 30-minute routine

| Time | Step |
|------|------|
| 7:00 | Glass of water |
| 7:02 | Make the bed |
| 7:05 | Stretch for 8 minutes |
| 7:15 | Shower and get ready |
| 7:30 | Breakfast without a screen |
| 7:40 | Write top three tasks |

## How to know it is working

After two weeks, ask: Do I do it on most days? Does it feel lighter, not heavier? If not, cut a step instead of pushing harder. Track it with a [habit tracker](/online-habit-tracker/) and see [how to stay consistent with habits](/blog/how-to-stay-consistent-with-habits/).
