Tracking a habit is easy to start and easy to do badly. People track too many things, define "done" vaguely, and then abandon the whole system the first time a streak breaks. This is a simple method that avoids those traps.

## 1. Track an action, not an outcome

"Lose 10 pounds" is an outcome. You cannot do it today. "Walk for 20 minutes" is an action. You either did it or you did not. Track the action and let the outcome follow. If you also care about the outcome, put it in a separate [goal](/goal-planner/) and review it monthly.

## 2. Decide exactly what counts as done

Before you track anything, finish this sentence: *"I will count today as done if ___."* Good answers are countable:

- Yes/No: "I took my medication."
- Count: "I drank 8 glasses of water."
- Duration: "I read for 30 minutes."
- Limit: "I had no more than 2 coffees."

If you would argue with yourself about whether a day counts, the definition is too loose.

## 3. Start with one to three habits

Every extra habit makes the daily check-in longer and the whole system easier to drop. Start small. You can add a habit later, and adding is a good sign: it means the existing ones are working. If you need ideas, shuffle the [habit ideas generator](/habit-ideas-generator/) or read [50 daily habit ideas](/blog/50-daily-habit-ideas/).

## 4. Track at a fixed moment

Pick one moment to log: right after the habit, or at the end of the day. A fixed moment turns tracking into part of the routine instead of one more thing to remember. If you track at night, keep the list short enough to finish in under a minute.

## 5. Make a missed day cheap

Everyone misses days. What matters is what the record does to you. Two practices help:

- **Log the miss honestly** instead of leaving it blank and hoping to forget it.
- **Aim never to miss twice.** One miss is noise. Two in a row starts a new pattern.

This is also why completion rate is a better scoreboard than a single streak. Twenty-seven days out of thirty is a strong month even if the longest streak is only twelve. The [habit streak calculator](/habit-streak-calculator/) and [consistency calculator](/habit-consistency-calculator/) show both.

## 6. Review weekly, not daily

Daily numbers are noisy. Once a week, spend five minutes asking:

1. Which habit has the most gaps?
2. Which day of the week do the gaps cluster on?
3. What got in the way: time, energy, forgetting, or the habit itself?
4. What is the smallest change that would help? Shrink it, move it or attach it to a different cue.

Change one thing at a time, then watch the next week.

## Which tool should you use?

Use the format you will actually open. A [free online tracker](/online-habit-tracker/) is a good place to try the method. A [printed weekly sheet](/printable-habit-tracker/) keeps it visible. An app like [Habon](/habit-tracker-for-iphone/) adds reminders and an Apple Watch app. Whatever you pick, the method above is the same.

## Quick checklist

- One to three habits
- A clear definition of done for each
- A fixed time to log
- Honest logging of misses
- A five-minute weekly review
