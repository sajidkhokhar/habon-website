A streak seems simple: count the days in a row. But a few details change the number, and it helps to know which ones.

## The three numbers

**Current streak.** The number of consecutive days you completed the habit, counting back from today. If you have not logged today yet, count back from yesterday. Otherwise every streak would show zero each morning.

**Longest streak.** The longest unbroken run anywhere in your history.

**Completion rate.** Days done divided by the days since your first completion.

## A worked example

You started on November 1 and today is November 20 (20 days). You completed it on these days: Nov 1 to 8 (8 days), then Nov 11 to 20 (10 days).

- Days done: 18
- Days since start: 20
- Missed days: 2 (Nov 9 and 10)
- Completion rate: 18 ÷ 20 = **90%**
- Longest streak: **10 days** (Nov 11 to 20)
- Current streak: **10 days**

You can check any set of dates with the [habit streak calculator](/habit-streak-calculator/).

## If today is not done yet

If you have not logged today but did yesterday, the streak is still alive: it only breaks at the end of the day. A good calculator counts back from yesterday in that case.

## Three types of streak

- **Calendar streak:** consecutive days. The harshest.
- **Schedule-aware streak:** counts only the days a habit is due. A Monday/Wednesday/Friday habit is not broken on Tuesday.
- **Period streak:** consecutive weeks or months that hit a target, such as three workouts a week.

Different tools use different rules. See [how streaks work](/streak-tracker/).

## Why completion rate matters

Two people can both have a 10-day streak. One has done the habit on 95% of the last 90 days. The other has done it on 40%. The streak alone hides that. Calculate both, and use the [consistency calculator](/habit-consistency-calculator/) to see how many perfect days you need to reach 80% or 90%.

## When a streak breaks

1. Restart today. A tiny version counts.
2. Look at your weekday pattern. Is the same day always missed?
3. Keep your longest streak in mind: it is a record you set and can beat.
4. Consider whether the habit needs to be smaller.

## Counting by hand

On a paper tracker, count the last unbroken run of filled circles for the current streak, scan for the longest run, and divide filled boxes by total days for the completion rate. A [printable monthly tracker](/printable-habit-tracker/) makes this easy.
